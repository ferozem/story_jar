Step 6: Anti-Repetition Rules
Before writing each story, check previous stories and vary:
•	Setting
•	Character type
•	Moral pressure
•	Humour type
•	Guide/resolution source
•	Ending pattern
Do not make every story happen in school.
Do not make every story end with a teacher explaining the lesson.
Do not always use lost objects, broken objects, competitions, or confession endings.
Do not always place humour at the beginning.
Use variety across the collection.
Step 7: Preferred Story Inspiration Transformations
Use the inspiration list as the source, but transform each one creatively.
Examples of valid transformation:
•	Hijrah cave → children separated on a nature trail, one stays calm and reassures the other.
•	Ta’if mercy → child forgives someone who mocked him and later helps that person.
•	Bedouin mosque incident → new child makes a messy mistake, others react harshly, main character corrects gently.
•	Ansari guest → child shares limited food with someone who forgot lunch.
•	Water passed to others → children keep offering water to one another on sports day.
•	Aishah and Umm Sulaym carrying water → child helps tired students during an event instead of chasing applause.
•	Talha’s sacrifice → child gives up chance to win in order to help someone.
•	Salamah’s chase → child notices a problem and acts quickly but safely.
•	Ka‘b ibn Malik → child tells the truth instead of making an easy excuse.
•	Hasan ibn Ali → child gives up leadership ego to make peace.
•	Abu Dharr corrected → child learns not to mock someone’s background, accent, clothes, or lunch.
•	Julaibib not forgotten → child notices and includes someone overlooked.
These examples are only direction. Create original plots, not mechanical copies.
Step 8: Story Quality Requirements
Each story must pass this test:
1.	Is it between 350 and 450 words?
2.	Does it begin with enough simple context?
3.	Is the language easy for children?
4.	Is there a clear moral problem?
5.	Does the main child make a meaningful choice?
6.	Is there at least one natural humorous moment?
7.	Is the humour believable and not cartoonish?
8.	Is the inspiration invisible?
9.	Are there no direct Islamic, religious, battlefield, or historical references in the child-facing story?
10.	Is the ending warm and satisfying?
11.	Is the moral clear and practical?
12.	Does it feel different from the previous stories?
If a story fails, revise it before moving to the next one.
