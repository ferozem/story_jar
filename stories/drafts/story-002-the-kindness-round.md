# The Kindness Round

## Story

Sami did not like speaking in front of the class. His voice always tried to hide somewhere behind his collar.

On Monday, he gave a short presentation about his model bridge. He had practiced all weekend, but when he said, "The bridge is strong because of triangles," Milo whispered, "The bridge is stronger than his voice."

A few children giggled. Sami's ears felt hot. He finished quickly and sat down, staring at his shoes.

Two days later, the school held a general knowledge contest. Sami and Milo both reached the final round. Anika called them the "fine-lists" by mistake, then bowed like she had invented an important new word. Even Sami laughed.

Before the final round began, Mrs. Dutta handed each finalist a card with three rules. Milo read his card, frowned, turned it upside down, and frowned harder.

"I don't understand the buzzer rule," he muttered.

Sami heard him. The rule was simple: press once, wait to be called, then answer. Milo was standing near the buzzer, already looking nervous.

Sami remembered the bridge comment. A small, sharp thought came into his mind: Let him mess up. Then everyone will laugh at him.

The thought did not feel as nice as he expected. It felt heavy.

Mrs. Dutta said, "Finalists, take your places."

Sami stepped closer to Milo and spoke quietly. "Press once only. If you press again before she calls you, the question passes to the next person."

Milo blinked. "Why are you telling me?"

Sami shrugged. "Because it is the rule."

The final round began. Milo answered one question correctly. Sami answered two. On the last question, Milo almost pressed twice, then stopped himself. Sami saw him take a careful breath.

Sami won by one point. Everyone clapped. Milo came over afterward and kicked the floor gently with his shoe.

"I was mean about your presentation," he said. "Sorry."

Sami nodded. "I was nervous."

"I know," Milo said. "I was nervous today."

Sami looked at his model bridge on the display table. It had needed all its pieces to stand. Maybe people were like that too.

## Moral of the Story

Forgiveness is stronger than payback, and helping someone can turn a hurt into a new beginning.
