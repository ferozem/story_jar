# The Spilled Paint Plan

## Story

The art room smelled like paper, glue, and the lemon soap Ms. Coral kept near the sink. Leela's class was making a giant poster for Family Night. Everyone had a job.

Leela painted tiny flowers around the border. Jay cut out stars. Ben, the new boy, was asked to carry the blue paint to Table Three.

"Shake well before use," Jay read from the bottle.

Ben looked worried. "How well?"

"Just a little," Leela said, but Jay had already turned back to his stars.

Ben held the bottle with both hands and shook it like a maraca. The lid was not closed properly. Blue paint splashed across the poster, the table, and one corner of Jay's sleeve.

For one second, everyone stared.

Then Jay shouted, "You ruined it!"

Ben's face went pale. "I thought it said shake well."

"Not like you are waking up a sleeping elephant!" Jay cried.

A few children groaned. Someone said they would have to start again. Ben stepped back, looking smaller and smaller.

Leela put down her brush. The poster did look bad. A blue river ran through the middle of the flowers, stars, and carefully written title. The poster was supposed to welcome every family, including Ben's. But shouting was not making the paint climb back into the bottle.

"Stop," Leela said. Her voice was not loud, but it was firm. "Ben did not know. We need paper towels."

Ms. Coral hurried over. Leela passed towels to Ben first. "Dab, don't rub," she said. "Rubbing makes it spread."

Jay muttered, "It already spread to next Tuesday."

Leela smiled a little. "Then we will make Tuesday useful."

She looked at the blue streak. "What if this becomes a river? The families can be standing on both sides, and the stars can go above it."

Ben looked up. "I can paint the bridge."

Jay frowned at his blue sleeve, then at the poster. "Fine. But no more elephant shaking."

By the end of class, the poster had a bright river, a bridge, stars, and flowers. It looked even more interesting than before.

Ben wrote his name very carefully in the corner with everyone else's.

## Moral of the Story

When someone makes a mistake, gentle help can fix more than shouting ever can.
