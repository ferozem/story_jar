# The Missing Thank-You

## Story

The school music evening ended with bright lights, loud clapping, and one banner that kept curling over the word WELCOME. Every time Mr. Hart fixed it, the corner rolled down again like it was shy.

Poppy had sung in the front row. Her cheeks hurt from smiling. Her family waved from the third row, and she felt proud enough to float. After the final song, the performers gathered on the stage for a thank-you photo.

"Choir in the middle!" called the photographer. "Drama group on the left! Dancers on the right!"

Everyone squeezed together. Poppy stood beside her friend Asha, who still had silver ribbon in her hair.

Then Poppy noticed Eli near the back wall. He was stacking chairs quietly. Eli had not performed, but he had spent the whole afternoon arranging seats, carrying music stands, and taping paper stars to the walls. He had also found three missing song sheets under a piano bench. When the microphone squealed before the show, he had found Mr. Hart in two seconds.

Now he was not in the photo.

"Smile!" said the photographer.

Poppy almost smiled. She liked being in the picture. Her grandparents would see it in the school newsletter, and she wanted them to spot her quickly.

But Eli lifted another chair and turned away.

"Wait!" Poppy said.

The photographer lowered the camera.

Poppy stepped forward. "Eli should be in it too."

Some children looked confused.

"He fixed the chairs and the stands," Poppy said. "And the microphone. And half the decorations."

Eli froze. "I am not dressed for a photo."

Asha looked at his shirt. "You have only one tape piece stuck to your sleeve. Very formal."

Everyone laughed kindly. Eli peeled off the tape and came forward, shy but smiling.

Mr. Hart nodded. "Poppy is right. A show needs helpers as much as performers."

They took the photo again. This time Eli stood near the front, holding one stubborn corner of the WELCOME banner so it would not curl.

In the picture, everyone looked happy, and the whole word WELCOME finally showed.

Poppy liked that photo best.

## Moral of the Story

Do not forget quiet helpers; everyone deserves to be seen and appreciated.
