# The Medal He Did Not Win

## Story

Leo's lucky socks did not match. One had blue stripes. The other had blue stripes too, but it had shrunk so much that it looked like it belonged to a very serious baby.

"They make me faster," Leo told Tara before the class relay.

"They make your ankles look confused," Tara said.

Leo grinned. He had practiced for three weeks. This was his race. He wanted the gold medal more than anything on the prize table, even the shiny pencil sharpener shaped like a rocket.

The relay began. Tara ran the second leg, and Leo waited for the baton. Their team was close to first place. Tara rounded the corner quickly. Then her foot slipped on a patch of loose sand near the track edge.

She fell hard.

The baton rolled away.

For a moment, everyone shouted at once. Leo saw the other runners fly past. If he grabbed the baton quickly, maybe he could still catch up. Tara pushed herself up, then sat back down, holding her knee.

"Go!" she said, trying to sound brave. "Run!"

Leo looked at the baton. He looked at Tara's face.

The medal seemed to move farther away, but Tara was right there.

Leo ran to her instead. "Can you stand?"

"I think so," Tara said, but her voice shook.

He waved to Coach Mina. "Tara is hurt!"

Coach hurried over. Leo picked up the baton and held it while Tara leaned on the coach. Their team finished last. The gold medals went to another group.

Leo's chest felt tight as he watched the winners clap. He wanted to be happy for them, but part of him felt like his tiny sock had squeezed his heart.

Then Tara came back with a bandage on her knee. "You gave up the race for me," she said.

Leo shrugged. "You looked more important than a medal."

At the end, Coach Mina called Leo forward. She did not give him a gold medal. She gave him the team ribbon for courage.

Leo pinned it beside his number card. His socks still looked silly, but he stood very tall.

## Moral of the Story

Winning is good, but helping someone in need is worth more than a prize.
