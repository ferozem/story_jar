# The Cart With the Wrong Box

## Story

Kiran was helping stack old cartons after the school charity drive. The good donation box was covered with bright stars and labeled WINTER SOCKS AND GLOVES. The recycling cartons were plain brown and smelled faintly of glue. The hallway was busy with chairs scraping and children carrying posters.

"Bright stars stay here," said Ms. Penny from the office. "Plain cartons go to the driveway."

Kiran repeated it in his head. Bright stars stay. Plain cartons go.

Then his shoelace came untied for the third time that morning.

"This lace wants a separate education," he muttered, kneeling to tie it.

While he was bent down, a helper rolled a cart past him. On top of the plain cartons sat the bright star donation box, tilted sideways under a flattened cereal box.

Kiran stood up fast. "Wait!"

The cart was already going through the side door toward the driveway. Kiran wanted to run after it and pull the box off himself, but the driveway had moving cars and delivery vans. The safety rule was clear: no students past the yellow line.

For one second, he wondered if someone else would notice. Adults were busy. Children were talking. The cart kept rolling.

Kiran ran to the office window instead. "Ms. Penny! The donation box is on the recycling cart!"

Ms. Penny looked up. "Are you sure?"

"Bright stars," Kiran said, pointing. "It is going outside."

Ms. Penny grabbed her walkie-talkie and called the caretaker. Kiran watched from behind the yellow line as the cart stopped near the van. The caretaker lifted the bright box from the top and carried it back.

"Good eyes," he told Kiran.

Kiran felt his face warm. "I almost chased it."

"But you did the safer thing," Ms. Penny said. "Quick thinking does not have to be risky."

When they opened the box, it was full of warm socks, gloves, and two knitted hats with bobbles so large they bounced like cheerful mushrooms.

Kiran tied his shoelace again, double knot this time. "You are not ruining any more missions," he told it.

The lace stayed quiet, which Kiran took as agreement.

## Moral of the Story

Being alert means noticing problems and getting the right help in a safe way.
