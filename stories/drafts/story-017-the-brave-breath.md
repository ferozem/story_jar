# The Brave Breath

## Story

Zain did not mind the clinic waiting room. It had fish stickers on the wall, a basket of books, and a chair that squeaked every time he moved. He named the chair Captain Squeak and tested it three times, just to be scientific.

He did mind the injection.

His mother sat beside him. "It will be quick."

"People say that about haircuts too," Zain said, "and haircuts take forever."

Across the room, a little girl hugged a stuffed bear and watched him. Her appointment card shook in her hand. Zain wanted to look brave, but his hands felt cold. When the nurse called his name, his legs forgot how standing worked.

In the small room, the nurse smiled. "Which arm, Zain?"

"Do I need both arms later?" he asked.

"Yes."

"Then I choose neither."

The nurse laughed gently. "I will be careful."

Zain climbed onto the chair. It squeaked. Captain Squeak had followed him. He wanted to shout, kick, or say the nurse was mean. But the little girl from the waiting room was peeking through the open door. Her eyes were wide.

If he panicked, she might feel more afraid. He knew fear could spread quickly, like spilled water running across a table.

Zain remembered how his mother told him to breathe slowly when thunder sounded too close. He put one hand on his stomach.

"I need a brave breath," he said.

"Good idea," said the nurse.

Zain breathed in like he was smelling warm soup and breathed out like he was cooling it. He did it twice. On the third breath, the nurse gave the injection.

It pinched. It was not nice. But it was already over.

"That was smaller than a haircut," Zain said, surprised.

Back in the waiting room, the little girl whispered, "Did it hurt?"

"A little," Zain said honestly. "But brave breaths help. Also, choose a chair with a good name."

The girl looked at Captain Squeak and smiled.

Zain's arm felt sore, but inside he felt steady. He had been scared and brave at the same time, which seemed impossible before and perfectly true now.

## Moral of the Story

Bravery does not mean you are never afraid; it means you face fear calmly and do what is needed.
